# EXP-008: a global LightGBM on the EXP-005 features is significantly worse than the GAM on 2024 (1114 against 912 MW)
Reference: EXP-005 | Date: 2026-10-06
Single change: replace the 48 per-slot GAMs of EXP-005 by one global LightGBM model with the local half-hour slot as a feature (M5 form B, modeling plan rung 3). Same input features, same weekly Monday refit, same expanding window from 2021-03-23, same missing-lag and missing-weather backups. Code: `src/load_forecast/gbm_model.py`, tests `tests/test_gbm_model.py`.

# Pre-registration (written and committed before any run of this model on 2023 or 2024 data)
Nothing of this model has been scored on any evaluation year. Only unit tests on synthetic data and a timing of LightGBM on random numbers (not on project data) were run.

Model:
- Features: exactly the EXP-005 frame of `lagged_gam_features` (dow_0 to dow_5, sin_1..3, cos_1..3, bridge, christmas_week, raw GFS national temperature `temperature_c`, lag_d2, lag_d7, lag_last and their three special-day indicators, 19 columns) plus `slot`, the Paris local half-hour index 0 to 47, as a plain numeric column (not categorical). The two occurrences of an autumn change slot share the same slot value.
- Training rows: every complete row known at the refit cutoff, from 2021-03-23 (the shared `training_matrix`, the same filter as the slot models). A day with a missing lag or weather has no row in training and gets NaN at forecast time, then the EXP-005 forecast, flagged `backup_exp005`, as in EXP-006 and EXP-007. LightGBM is never allowed to route a NaN.
- Objective: `l1` (MAE is scored). Leaf values are renewed with the median by LightGBM for this objective.
- Fixed and not tuned: bagging fraction 1, feature fraction 1, L1 and L2 regularisation 0, max_bin 255 (default), `seed` 20240, `deterministic` true, row-wise histograms. No early stopping (it would need a validation block). The result does not depend on the number of threads.
- Refit: weekly at the Monday cutoff, expanding window, exactly as the slot models; one booster per refit serves all 48 slots.

Hyperparameters (M10 A, D13): scored on the 2023 inner walk-forward only (`scripts/select_lightgbm.py`, consumption deleted after 2023-12-31, weather read to 2023-12-31, refits from 2021-03-23).
- Grid, fixed here: learning rate {0.03, 0.1} x num_leaves {15, 63} x min_data_in_leaf {20, 100} x number of rounds {100, 200, 400, 800, 1600} = 40 candidates. The five round counts of a triple come from prefixes of the same 1600-tree booster, which equals the booster trained for that many rounds (tested).
- Score: MAE over all 2023 slots with a forecast and an actual value (the same convention as the EXP-005 smoothing choice, where the plain GAM scored 1057.0 MW on 2023; that figure is context only and plays no role in the rule).
- Rule: choose the candidate with the lowest 2023 MAE. If several are within 1.0 MW of the lowest, take among them the one with the fewest leaves, then the fewest rounds, then the larger min_data_in_leaf, then the larger learning rate. The 2024 run is made whatever the 2023 result, including when LightGBM does not beat the GAM in 2023.
- Mean cost of this grid: one fit takes 5 to 11 seconds on 65000 random rows, so a triple (52 weekly fits) takes some minutes and the whole grid about an hour.

What I will report for 2024 (full evaluation checklist from original contract section 6, nothing from 2025):
- MAE, RMSE, MAPE of EXP-008, EXP-005 (reproduced in the same run), EXP-007 (reproduced in the same run), raw RTE D-1 and RTE* a2, on common slots.
- MAE by day type (ordinary, cold, holiday, bridge, DST) against RTE and RTE*, slot-weighted.
- Paired daily-MAE differences with the 7-day block bootstrap (14-day as a check, 10000 draws, seed 20240) against EXP-005, EXP-007, raw RTE D-1 and RTE* a2. If an interval contains 0, "not significant".
- Monthly mean error and MAE.
- Cold-day extrapolation (the known limit of trees, modeling plan rung 3): for each 2024 cold day, whether its lowest forecast temperature is below the lowest temperature of all days before the refit Monday of that week (the training range), and the mean signed error of EXP-008 on cold days against EXP-005. Descriptive only, 22 cold days.
- Not reported: interval coverage (point forecast).

By the pre-registered rule the grid winner is lr 0.1, 15 leaves, min_data 100, 1600 rounds (1294.4 MW). No other candidate is within 1.0 MW of it, so the tie rule does not act. Two things stand out and I report them as they are:
- The winner sits on three edges of the grid at once (the largest round count, the smallest leaf count, the largest min_data), and every triple was still improving at 1600 rounds (the drop from 800 to 1600 is 20 to 55 MW). So the grid does not contain the optimum of this model, and the best 2023 score of 1294 MW is 237 MW worse than the GAM on the same year. Part of the reason is the L1 objective: its gradient is only the sign of the error, so the boosting steps are small relative to the 30 to 80 GW range of the level, and many rounds are needed (a guess about the cause, not tested).
- I decided to extend the grid after seeing these 2023 numbers. This is a deviation from the pre-registration, not a hidden one: the design said the grid is fixed, and I now add a second stage. It uses 2023 only (D13), so nothing from 2024 influences it.

Stage 2 (declared here, committed before it is run): re-run the two best triples of stage 1 at 1600 rounds, (0.1, 15, 100) and (0.1, 15, 20), with 3200 and 6400 rounds (`select_lightgbm.py extend`), and apply the same rule to the union of the 44 candidates. If the winner is still at 6400 rounds I stop there, keep it, and say in the conclusion that the model is limited by the grid. The other two edges (fewer than 15 leaves, min_data above 100) are not extended: this is a cost limit I accept and mention as a caveat.

# 2023 choice, stage 2 (recorded in its own commit, before any 2024 run)
`select_lightgbm.py extend` was run once (about 25 minutes; stage 1 log `data/select_lightgbm.log`, stage 2 log `data/select_lightgbm_stage2.log`). Same 17422 slots. 2023 MAE in MW:

| lr | leaves | min_data | 1600 rounds (stage 1) | 3200 | 6400 |
|----|--------|----------|-----------------------|------|------|
| 0.1 | 15 | 100 | 1294.4 | 1279.4 | **1271.1** |
| 0.1 | 15 | 20 | 1299.7 | 1290.8 | 1287.3 |

Over the 44 candidates the lowest 2023 MAE is 1271.07 MW (lr 0.1, 15 leaves, min_data 100, 6400 rounds); the next one is 1279.4 MW, so the tie rule does not act. The choice is **`lr=0.1, leaves=15, min_data=100, rounds=6400`**.

Remarks, as read from the numbers:
- Doubling the rounds still lowers the score (-15 MW from 1600 to 3200, -8 MW from 3200 to 6400): the gains shrink but have not stopped, so the winner is still on the round edge. By the rule declared above I stop here. The model is limited by the grid and by what I accepted to spend, not by a proven optimum.
- The 2023 MAE of the chosen LightGBM (1271 MW) is 214 MW above the plain GAM on the same year (1057 MW). Whatever happens in 2024, this model starts well behind on the year it was tuned on.
- The two other edges (15 leaves, min_data 100) are not explored further.

EXP-008 is below EXP-005 in one month only (August, -70 MW) and above it in the 11 others. The monthly mean error of EXP-008 swings by about 1.6 GW between January (+761) and February (-837), against a swing of about 0.4 GW for EXP-005 between those two months: the level of the forecast is less stable (a description, not an explanation).

Cold-day extrapolation (the known limit of trees, modeling plan rung 3), descriptive, 22 cold days: only 2 of them (2024-01-09 and 2024-01-20) have a daily minimum of the national forecast temperature below the lowest temperature of all days before their refit Monday, and by 0.05 and 0.25 deg C. The 2024 cold days are therefore not colder than the training days, and this year does not test the extrapolation limit. The mean signed error on cold days is +7 MW for EXP-008 against -908 MW for EXP-005 and -959 MW for EXP-007: the GAMs under-forecast the cold days by about 0.9 GW on average, the LightGBM is on average on target but its mean daily MAE on those days is 2170 MW (EXP-005: 1819), so its cold-day error is spread in both directions, not a one-sided miss.
Not tested: why the LightGBM is behind on every day type; whether a model with more rounds, fewer leaves or a larger min_data (the three edges of the grid) would close the gap; a LightGBM fitted on the GAM residuals (the plan's option for later); a different treatment of the slot (categorical) or of the lags.

Conclusion: the global LightGBM does not beat the GAM on 2024. Its MAE is 1114 MW against 912 MW for EXP-005, 202 MW worse with a 7-day interval [+120, +292] that excludes 0, and it is significantly worse than RTE* (+217 MW) while still significantly better than raw RTE D-1 (-250 MW). The same gap was seen on 2023, the year of the tuning (1271 MW against 1057 MW for the GAM, +214 MW), so the loss is consistent over two years and is not a 2024 accident. The tuned model sat at the edge of its grid in rounds, leaves and min_data, so a better configuration may exist; I did not look for it and make no claim about what an optimum would reach. EXP-005 stays the best base model, EXP-007 the best validated model.
Caveats that matter: (1) the revision leak of the lags is unchanged from EXP-004 and the model reads the same consolidated values (open question in my private notes). (2) The grid was extended once after seeing 2023 scores (stage 2, declared and committed before it ran, 2023 only), so the pre-registered grid was not followed to the letter. (3) The configuration is on the edge of the grid in three parameters (6400 rounds, 15 leaves, min_data 100). (4) The cold-day extrapolation limit was not tested because the 2024 cold days are not colder than the training days. (5) One seed (20240), deterministic; nothing about seed variance.
