# EXP-005: a smooth temperature effect cuts the 2024 MAE from 1016 to 912 MW and ties the leak-free RTE* benchmark, but still rests on consolidated lags
Reference: EXP-004 | Date: 2026-10-06
Single change: in the EXP-004 model, replace the two degree-day features (hdd, cdd with fixed bases 15 and 20 C) by the D11 GFS national temperature entering as a penalized cubic B-spline (M4 option C, M6 A: statsmodels `GLMGam`, `src/load_forecast/gam_model.py`). Every other feature is identical and enters linearly: day of week (holiday as Sunday), Fourier terms of order 3, bridge, Christmas week, lag_d2, lag_d7, lag_last and the three special-day indicators. One GAM per Paris local half-hour slot (48 models), expanding window from 2021-03-23, weekly Monday refit, walk-forward runner. Outside the training temperature range the curve continues linearly with its boundary slope.
Missing rule, as in EXP-004: a day with a missing lag or no weather gets NaN from the GAM (`missing_as_nan` option), takes the EXP-004 forecast and is flagged `backup_exp004`. In 2024 that is 2 days, 96 slots (2024-04-02, 2024-04-07), the days whose D-2 and D-7 lag day is the 23-hour spring change day. The weather-missing day 2022-12-01 is outside 2024.

Smoothing rule, written before the 2024 GAM run (D13, M10 A):
- Fixed in advance, never tuned: 8 degrees of freedom for the temperature spline, cubic, one smooth term.
- Chosen on 2023 only: one penalty weight shared by the 48 slot models, taken from the decade grid 0.1 to 1e6 by the lowest MAE of an inner walk-forward over 2023 (weekly refits from 2021-03-23, same features and runner). `scripts/select_gam_smoothing.py` deletes all consumption after 2023-12-31 before the runner starts. Result (17422 slots each): 0.1: 1064.2; 1: 1063.9; 10: 1062.4; **100: 1057.0**; 1e3: 1060.5; 1e4: 1256.7; 1e5: 1442.7; 1e6: 1474.2 MW. The best value is inside the grid, so the grid was not extended. The weight 100 is frozen in `scripts/run_exp005.py` (`SMOOTHING_WEIGHT`).
- Why one weight: the runner builds all slot models from one factory, a single weight is easy to explain and cannot over-fit 48 separate choices. The curve is flat from 0.1 to 1e3 (a 7 MW spread), so the choice matters little; only values from 1e4 on over-smooth.
- Nothing else tuned. Features, lags, bases of EXP-004 and the backup rule unchanged.

Backtest period: 2024-01-01 to 2024-12-31 inclusive, Europe/Paris. Consumption from a development build of the clean model `load_30min` up to 2024-12-31 22:30 UTC, weather for delivery days 2021-03-24 to 2024-12-31 (no `not_collected` day, checked by the script). Nothing from 2025.

The GAM is below EXP-004 in all 12 months and below raw RTE in every month except August (958 against 869 MW) and December (1642 against 1588 MW). The mean error stays within 340 MW of zero in every month; February (-340), November (-333) and July (-249) are the largest.

Fitted temperature curves (figure `docs/experiments/EXP-005_temperature_curves.png`, MLflow artifact `plots/EXP-005_temperature_curves.png`): effect on load relative to 15 C, at three slots, from the models of the last 2024 refit (Monday 2024-12-30 cutoff, descriptive only). The GAM keeps the degree-day shape: a steep cold branch, a flat zone and a gentler hot branch. It differs from the fixed bases in three ways. It rounds the corner at 15 C instead of a kink. It dips slightly below zero between 15 and 20 C at noon and in the evening (about -1000 MW near 19 C), so the lowest point of the curve is near 19 C, not at the 15 C base. And between about 2 and 14 C it lies below the straight HDD line (at 03:00 it is above it in the coldest range). Boundary slopes (MW per C, cold side): -1046 at 03:00, -1166 at 12:00, -1167 at 19:00; the HDD coefficients of EXP-004 for the same slots are 728, 1045 and 1237 MW per C. The hot side is flatter than the linear CDD at noon and in the evening.

Conclusion: letting the model learn the shape of the temperature effect lowers the 2024 MAE by 104 MW per day (a 10 percent cut, significant under 7 and 14-day blocks), ordinary days and all months improve, and the model is significantly better than raw RTE D-1 (-453 MW per day). Against the leak-free benchmark RTE* the difference is +15 MW with an interval that contains 0, so I write "not significant": I cannot say the model beats RTE*, only that it is no longer distinguishable from it on 2024. Ordinary days are slightly below RTE* (819 against 847 MW, descriptive), cold days and holidays are above it (1819 against 1489 and 1843 against 1187 MW, descriptive, 22 and 11 days).
Caveats that matter: (1) the revision leak of EXP-004 is unchanged: lags are consolidated values in the backtest and real-time values in production, and the lag coefficients (about 0.57 in total in the linear model, not re-measured in the GAM) pass an offset into the forecast. Raw RTE is the weaker comparison; RTE* is the fair one. (2) Selecting between experiments on 2024 is still a selection on 2024 (M10): the penalty weight came from 2023 only, but the decision to move from degree-days to a GAM is read on 2024. (3) The 2023 inner MAE (1057 MW) is a different year with a shorter training window and is not comparable to the 2024 figure. (4) Holiday and cold-day gaps to RTE* remain, and the Kalman rung (EXP-007) is the planned answer for level errors; nothing here shows it will help.
Limits: the temperature enters through one national mean; the smooth is the same shape for every day of the week; 8 degrees of freedom were fixed in advance and not varied. The EXP-006 smoothed-temperature term is not tested here.

Leakage check: the feature builder is the EXP-004 builder with the degree features replaced by the temperature of the same selected run, read only at the slots of the day asked for. `tests/test_gam_lagged.py` removes every consumption value after the last usable slot and requires identical `walk_forward` GAM predictions for three days (a Monday refit and two later days), checks that days before the first weather day and days with a missing lag or no weather give NaN instead of an error, and that the strict mode still refuses NaN. The lag leakage mutants stay in `tests/test_lag_model.py`. The penalty weight never saw 2024: `select_gam_smoothing.py` deletes later consumption before the runner starts and reads weather only to 2023-12-31.
