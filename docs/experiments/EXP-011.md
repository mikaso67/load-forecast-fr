# EXP-011: adaptive conformal inference on the EXP-010 intervals to fix the seasonal miscalibration
Reference: EXP-010 (same point forecast, same window N = 182, same residuals, static quantile level) | Date: 2026-10-07
Single change: the quantile level of the rolling split-conformal interval of each local slot is no longer fixed at 80 percent but adapted online with the adaptive conformal inference update of Gibbs and Candes (2021). The point forecast, the window N = 182 days and the residuals are not changed. Code: `adaptive_conformal_intervals` in `src/load_forecast/conformal.py`, tests `tests/test_conformal.py`, choice of the step `scripts/select_aci_step.py`.

# Pre-registration (written and committed before any adaptive interval was computed on project data)
Only unit tests on synthetic data were run. No adaptive interval and no step choice has been computed on 2023 or 2024 project data. What I had already seen when I wrote this: the EXP-010 results on 2024 (winter 58.7 percent, cold days 40.3 percent, by month in `EXP-010.md`), which motivate the experiment. The step is chosen on 2023 only.

Point forecast: the EXP-009 shared-weight ML-Poly aggregate, unchanged. In the final run its 2024 MAE must be reproduced to 0.01 MW (828.52), the four experts to 0.01 MW (1015.70, 911.85, 885.32, 1113.94) by recomputing them as in `scripts/run_exp010.py`, and the EXP-010 intervals (static quantile level, N = 182) must be reproduced slot by slot from the same run (maximum difference under 1e-6 MW against `data/experiments/EXP-010/intervals.csv`).

Method, per local half-hour slot s (48 independent states):
- State alpha_t[s], starting at alpha = 0.2 (1 minus the 80 percent level) at the start of the residual series (2023-01-01) and updated as alpha_{t+1} = alpha_t + gamma * (alpha - err_t), with err_t the miscoverage of the interval issued for that day and slot (1 if the actual is outside it, 0 if inside). Residuals, window and update calendar come from the 2023 burn-in aggregate then from 2024, without reset.
- Interval for delivery day D: the window of the residuals of slot s over the N = 182 local days D-2-N+1 to D-2, as in EXP-010, with the two tails at alpha_t / 2 each: lower order statistic of rank floor((n+1) * alpha_t / 2), upper order statistic of rank ceil((n+1) * (1 - alpha_t / 2)), n the number of finite residuals of the window.
- Choices not given by the paper, fixed here: (1) The tail alpha_t / 2 is clipped to [0, 0.5] and the ranks to 1..n, so a state below 0 gives the widest finite interval of the window (its minimum and maximum residual) and a state above 1 gives a very narrow one, never an infinite or inverted interval. The state alpha_t itself is not clipped, as in the paper. (2) One update per local day and slot: err_t is the share of that day's occurrences of the slot outside their interval (an autumn repeated slot has two, so err can be 0.5), a spring slot that does not exist has no update, a missing actual has no update. (3) A day and slot whose window has fewer than 9 finite residuals gets no interval and no update (the same rule as EXP-010, so the two experiments are defined on the same slots). (4) The two-sided symmetric level is adapted, not each tail separately.
- Information rule (D2): the interval for D uses the actuals of local days up to D-2 only, both in the window and in the state. The error of day d is absorbed by the state of day d+2 and later, never by D-1. The error of day d is computed with the interval that was issued for d with the state of d (it never uses a recomputed interval).
- gamma = 0 reproduces EXP-010 exactly (unit test).
- Truncation test (`tests/test_conformal.py::test_adaptive_interval_of_day_d_ignores_actuals_after_d_minus_2`): deleting every actual after D-2 leaves the interval of D identical, for each day D; `test_an_adaptive_variant_that_absorbs_d_minus_1_fails_the_truncation_check` shows the same check fails when the window and the state absorb D-1. The state recursion is also re-derived independently from the output in `test_state_is_alpha_plus_gamma_times_the_errors_of_days_up_to_d_minus_2`.

Choice of gamma, on 2023 only, `scripts/select_aci_step.py`:
- Grid fixed here: gamma in {0.001, 0.005, 0.01, 0.05}. N = 182 days is not tuned again.
- Data: the cached 2023 expert forecasts (`data/experiments/EXP-009/experts_2023.csv`), aggregated as in EXP-009 (shared variant), residuals against the 2023 consolidated actuals, the adaptive recursion started on 2023-01-01.
- Scored slots: the slots EXP-010 scored on 2023, delivery days 2023-07-03 to 2023-12-31 (the longest window is full), identical for every gamma. gamma = 0 (that is EXP-010) is printed for context and is not a candidate.
- Rule, the one of EXP-010: the gamma whose coverage is closest to 80 percent; if several gammas are within 0.5 percentage point of the best distance, the one with the smallest mean width. Printed for context: the block bootstrap interval of the coverage and the coverage by month.
- Caveat stated now: the 2023 residuals come from experts tuned on 2023 and the scored half year July to December has no cold-season onset comparable with January 2024, so 2023 may not be able to tell the steps apart (as for N in EXP-010). If the coverages of the four steps differ by less than their bootstrap intervals, the rule decides anyway and I say so.
- The choice is recorded in this file in its own commit, with the sentence "the chosen step is **gamma=X**", before any 2024 run. `scripts/run_exp011.py` refuses to start unless that commit is in the history and names the step used.
- No dry run on 2024 before the final run. The script is only exercised on synthetic data and on 2023 until the final run. If the final run fails after it has printed 2024 numbers, I fix the failure without changing any setting, rerun, and disclose both runs here.
- The 2024 run is made whatever the 2023 result.

What I will report for 2024 (full evaluation checklist from original contract section 6, nothing from 2025), side by side with EXP-010 recomputed in the same run:
- Point forecast MAE, RMSE, MAPE against RTE D-1 and RTE* a2 on common slots (reproduction check of EXP-009) and MAE by day type.
- Overall coverage of the 80 percent interval with a 95 percent moving block bootstrap interval (7-day blocks, 10000 draws, seed 20240; 14-day check), against 78 to 82 percent.
- Coverage and mean width by season, by day type (cold with D9 labels, holiday, ordinary; bridge and DST descriptive), by month, and the share of days with every slot covered.
- Mean interval width in MW and pinball loss at 0.1, 0.5 and 0.9.
- Paired comparison with EXP-010 on the same timestamps: difference of the overall coverage (EXP-011 minus EXP-010) with a 7-day block bootstrap interval; the mean state alpha_t by month, descriptive.
- Decision rule stated now: I call the by-season calibration met only if the coverage of each of the four seasons is inside 78 to 82 percent; otherwise not met, whatever the overall figure. I call EXP-011 better than EXP-010 on overall coverage only if the paired interval of the overall difference excludes 0; a wider interval that is not sharper is a cost I report through the width and the pinball losses.
- Expectation written before the run, to be confirmed or refuted: the adaptive level should raise winter coverage and lower the spring and summer over-coverage, because the state reacts to the misses of the previous days. It cannot fix the cold days and holidays by design: the state is per slot and does not know the day type, and the update arrives two days late, so a short event of a few days is over before the state moves. The long-run coverage guarantee of the paper assumes immediate feedback, here the feedback is delayed by two days, so I claim no guarantee.

# 2023 choice (recorded in its own commit, before any 2024 run)
`scripts/select_aci_step.py` was run once on 2023 after the pre-registration commit 2c162a7 (log `data/select_aci_step.log`). Scored slots: 8736 slots, 182 days, 2023-07-03 to 2023-12-31, identical for every step. Coverage of the 80% interval with its 95% 7-day block bootstrap interval, and mean width:

Coverage by month (Jul to Dec), EXP-010 / gamma 0.001 / gamma 0.05: Jul 92.3 / 91.7 / 69.3, Aug 81.9 / 79.8 / 69.7, Sep 97.3 / 96.7 / 90.7, Oct 84.8 / 83.3 / 72.1, Nov 68.1 / 65.8 / 71.9, Dec 46.4 / 45.0 / 73.7 (percent).

By the rule, gamma = 0.001 is the closest to 80 percent among the candidates (3.2 points) and no other step is within 0.5 point of it, so the chosen step is **gamma=0.001**.

Reading, 2023 only, nothing here is a 2024 result:
- The rule selects the smallest step of the grid, an edge of the grid, and the result is a step so small that the state barely moves (alpha_t stays between 0.188 and 0.23 over 2023). The adaptive interval with gamma = 0.001 is therefore almost the EXP-010 interval, slightly narrower (2234 against 2333 MW) and slightly less covering (76.8 against 78.3 percent). I do not extend the grid after seeing this: the grid was fixed before the run and the rule decides.
- The coverage of a step is not monotone in gamma (76.8, 72.7, 70.8, 74.6), and the four bootstrap intervals overlap widely (they are 11 to 15 points wide), so the 2023 half year cannot separate the steps by coverage.
- The largest step does what the method is meant to do in the cold months (Nov 71.9, Dec 73.7 against 68.1 and 46.4 for EXP-010) but pays for it in July and August (69 percent) after the state has accumulated the over-coverage of the previous months (integral windup of the update). This is a pattern in 2023 H2, noted for the interpretation of the 2024 results, not a selection argument: the rule was fixed in advance and picks 0.001.
- Check of the implementation on 2023 only, not a selection step: over all 2023 days with a defined interval (355 days per slot), the worst per-slot deviation of the miss rate from 0.2 is 0.039 for gamma = 0.001 and 0.014 for gamma = 0.05, the second under the bound (max(alpha, 1 - alpha) + gamma) / (gamma * T) = 0.048 of the paper for immediate feedback (here the feedback is delayed, so it is a consistency check, not a guarantee). For gamma = 0.001 the bound is 2.3, the guarantee says nothing at that step with one year of daily data per slot.

State: the mean alpha_t by month stays between 0.198 (February) and 0.217 (October) and its overall range is 0.186 to 0.232, so the adaptive level moved by at most 0.032 from its start value 0.2 over 2023 and 2024.

Difference vs reference (EXP-010), paired on the same 17566 timestamps: overall coverage difference -0.44 points, 95% 7-day block bootstrap interval [-0.69, -0.22], 14-day check [-0.76, -0.15]. The interval excludes 0: the adaptive interval covers slightly less than the static one, and it is 24 MW (0.9 percent) narrower. This is a statistically detectable but practically tiny difference, and it goes in the wrong direction for the coverage target.
By-season calibration: not met (only autumn, 78.0 percent, is inside 78 to 82 percent, as for EXP-010; winter is 58.8 percent). Overall coverage 78.1 percent has a 7-day interval [73.5, 83.5] that is not inside 78 to 82, so, as for EXP-010, the evidence does not show overall calibration either.
Expectation written before the run, refuted: I expected the adaptive level to raise winter coverage and lower spring and summer over-coverage; winter did not move (58.7 to 58.8) and summer fell by 0.7 points only. The part of the expectation that said cold days and holidays would not be fixed by design is confirmed (39.9 and 41.7 percent).
Conclusion: with the step chosen on 2023 by the pre-registered rule (gamma = 0.001), adaptive conformal inference is almost the static interval: the state moves by about 0.03 at most and the coverage by season does not change. This tests the selected step, not adaptive conformal inference in general. The 2023 grid did show that a larger step (gamma = 0.05) lifts the November and December 2023 coverage from 68 and 46 percent to 72 and 74 percent, at the cost of July and August; the same step has not been run on 2024 and will not be tuned on 2024. The seasonal failure of EXP-010 remains open: it needs either a mechanism that reacts within days (a larger step, with its windup cost, validated on a period that contains a cold onset), or residuals pooled or scaled by season and day type.
Caveats: (1) the step was selected on 2023 H2 where the four coverages are not separable (bootstrap intervals 11 to 15 points wide) and the rule picked the smallest step, an edge of the grid; (2) the 2023 residuals come from experts tuned on 2023; (3) residuals use consolidated actuals (open revision question in my private notes); (4) a single year, winter 2024 is 91 days with strong autocorrelation, per-season intervals were not computed; (5) the update arrives two days late by D2, so the long-run guarantee of the method, which assumes immediate feedback, is not claimed.
