# EXP-014: using the part of D-1 already known at the cutoff lowers the 2024 error of EXP-009, by 26 MW with a level term and by 15 MW with a faster Kalman update
Reference: EXP-009 (ML-Poly aggregation of EXP-004, EXP-005, EXP-007, EXP-008) | Date: 2026-11-05
Single change: the online layer (aggregation weights, Kalman state or a level term) also uses the consumption of D-1 that is already known at the 12:00 D-1 cutoff. One variant at a time, everything else frozen as in EXP-009. Code: `src/load_forecast/aggregation.py` (`aggregate(..., morning=True)`, `morning_level_shift`), `src/load_forecast/kalman.py` (`walk_forward_kalman(..., morning=True)`), tests in `tests/test_aggregation.py` and `tests/test_kalman.py`, script `scripts/run_exp014.py`. Production is not changed: the new switches are off by default and no production module passes them.

# Pre-registration (written and committed before any run of the variants on 2023 or 2024 data)

## Question
The online layer reacts two days late: the aggregation weights and the Kalman state for day D absorb full days up to D-2 only. Live, the errors swung at the first heating week of autumn 2026 while RTE D-1 stayed close to zero (`docs/analysis_live_level_shift.md`). At the cutoff of D, the D2 rule already gives the consumption of D-1 from 00:00 to 09:30 Paris (slots ending at or before 10:00, two hours before the cutoff): 20 slots on an ordinary day, 18 after the spring change night, 22 after the autumn one. Does feeding these slots to the online layer reduce the 2024 error?

Note on what the experts already see: EXP-004, EXP-005, EXP-007 and EXP-008 already take the D-1 09:30 value as an input (the "last known value" lag of EXP-004). The variants below do not add an input to any expert; they change only how fast the online layer learns from its own recent errors.

## Known slots (the D2 cutoff, unchanged)
For delivery day D, a slot of D-1 is known when its UTC start is at or before `last_usable_slot_start(D)`, the same function that cuts the history of every expert. No consumption after 09:30 Paris on D-1 is read. Tests check this for every variant by running the forecast of D on a history cut at that slot (or with every later value changed) and requiring an identical forecast, including on days next to both clock changes. Other tests check that each variant really reacts to the known D-1 slots, so the leakage tests are not passing by accident.

## The three variants (no other variant will be run)
- **A1, aggregation with the morning losses.** In the shared ML-Poly aggregation of EXP-009, at the cutoff of D the known slots of D-1 are absorbed as a round of their own, with the weights that issued the D-1 forecast; the rest of D-1 is absorbed at the cutoff of D+1 as before. The regrets summed over a full day are unchanged; what changes is that part of them arrives one day earlier, and that a day now counts as two rounds in the squared-regret learning rate of ML-Poly. No parameter.
- **A2, Kalman with the morning slots.** In EXP-007 (restart mode, variances fitted at the first refit of each quarter on days up to the refit minus 2 days, unchanged), the forecast of D uses a copy of the filter that also absorbs the known slots of D-1. Each local half-hour slot has its own filter, so only the slots of D from 00:00 to 09:30 Paris can change; the running filter still absorbs full days up to D-2. The new expert replaces EXP-007 in the EXP-009 aggregation, both in the 2023 burn-in and in 2024; the three other experts are the frozen EXP-009 forecasts. No parameter.
- **A3, level term.** The EXP-009 aggregate for D is shifted by `-lambda * e`, where `e` is the mean error (aggregate issued for D-1 minus actual) over the known slots of D-1. A day without a known slot is not shifted.

## Choice made on 2023 only (D13)
One choice: the shrinkage `lambda` of A3, on the grid 0, 0.1, ..., 1.0. Rule: take the smallest `lambda` whose 2023 MAE is within 1.0 MW of the lowest 2023 MAE of the grid. If the rule gives 0, A3 is EXP-009 itself: I record it and do not run A3 on 2024.

2023 data: the frozen 2023 expert forecasts of the EXP-009 burn-in (`data/experiments/EXP-009/experts_2023.csv`, weekly refits from 2021-03-23, consumption deleted after 2023-12-31, weather read to 2023-12-31), the EXP-009 aggregate on 2023 (uniform weights on 2023-01-01, it must reproduce the 2023 MAE of 844.65 MW), and for A2 the EXP-007 walk-forward on 2023 rerun with the morning switch on the same cut data.

`run_exp014.py select` also reports the 2023 MAE of A1 and A2. These numbers choose nothing: A1 and A2 are run on 2024 whatever their 2023 result.

Reproduction checks (the run stops if one fails): EXP-009 2023 MAE 844.65 and 2024 MAE 828.52 MW to 0.01 MW from the frozen expert forecasts, each 2024 expert to 0.01 MW of its own experiment; in A2, the GAM equals the frozen EXP-005 and every slot from 10:00 Paris on equals the frozen EXP-007, both to 0.01 MW per slot.

## What I will report for 2024 (nothing from 2025)
- MAE, RMSE, MAPE of each variant, of EXP-009, of raw RTE D-1 and of RTE* a2 on the common slots (17,566 slots for EXP-009).
- MAE by day type (ordinary, cold, holiday, bridge, DST), slot-weighted, against raw RTE D-1 and RTE*.
- **Primary test, one per variant:** paired daily MAE difference with EXP-009 (variant minus EXP-009), 7-day moving block bootstrap, 10,000 draws, seed 20240, 14-day blocks as a check. If the 7-day interval contains 0, "not significant". There are up to three tests; I do not adjust the intervals, but I write that one variant is useful only if its 7-day interval excludes 0 and the 14-day check agrees. Even then this experiment changes nothing in production: a production change would be a separate decision.
- **Secondary, descriptive only (no verdict):**
  - the window 2024-10-01 to 2024-11-30 (the autumn heating onset): mean paired daily difference with its 7-day block interval computed with the other days left out, and the mean signed error of the variant and of EXP-009;
  - transition days: days of 2024 whose observed national mean temperature (D9 SYNOP labels) differs by more than 3 deg C from the mean of the 7 days before (at least 5 of them labelled); same statistics. The labels are evaluation data, never an input.
  - for A2, the new Kalman expert alone against EXP-007, paired by day.
- MLflow: one run per variant with the tags `author`, `exp` = EXP-014, `backtest_start`, `backtest_end`.
- No 2025 read: the script asserts that no consumption, weather or forecast timestamp reaches 2025-01-01 Paris, on top of the period lock of the runners.
- If no variant helps, I say so plainly.

Conclusion: the consumption of D-1 that is already known at the cutoff carries information the online layer did not use. A level term shrunk to 0.3 lowers the 2024 MAE of EXP-009 from 828.5 to 802.8 MW (-25.6 MW [-42.6, -5.8]), and the morning Kalman update from 828.5 to 813.8 MW (-14.7 MW [-19.6, -8.4]); the morning losses in the aggregation change almost nothing. The A3 gain came out smaller than on 2023 (-30 MW), as expected; the A2 gain came out larger (-11 MW on 2023).

Caveats:
1. Revision gap. In this backtest the D-1 morning values are consolidated values (accepted leak 12 of the protocol). In production they are real-time values mapped to the consolidated scale (D21). A3 subtracts 0.3 times a mean error measured on those values, so a mapping error of 1,000 MW would move the forecast by 300 MW; A2 is exposed in the same way on the morning slots. The gain is shown only on the consolidated scale.
2. Not combined. A2 and A3 were tested separately; whether they add up is not known.
3. The shrinkage was chosen on a single year whose error was a steady over-forecast, and validated on one year. The 2023 grid is flat between 0.2 and 0.4 (within 5 MW), so the exact value matters little.
4. 2025 was not used. This experiment changes nothing in production.
