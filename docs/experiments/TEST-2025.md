## 3. What moves, as in production
- Experts: one continuous walk-forward from 2024-01-01 to 2025-12-31, expanding window from 2021-03-23, refit every Monday on data known at that Monday's cutoff, features built at each day's own cutoff (D2 slot-end rule). The Kalman variance warm start therefore continues across the year boundary.
- Aggregate: the weights of 2025-01-01 are the ones reached by absorbing the days up to 2024-12-30 (D-2 rule), carried without reset, then updated daily with D-2 losses.
- Intervals: the pool rolls with the same D-2 rule. Residuals are those of the aggregate from 2024-01-01 onward, out of sample. The 2023 burn-in residuals are not used. This differs from EXP-012 by one day only: on 2025-01-01 the 365-day window starts on 2023-12-31, which holds a 2023 residual that EXP-012 would have used.
- Consolidated actuals are used for lags, aggregation losses and residuals (the revision gap of my open questions is unchanged, and no real-time comparison is made, D15).

Weather: `read_temperature` up to 2025-12-31 only. SYNOP labels for 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31 only. Nothing after 2025-12-31 is read by any query of the script. Cold-day labels: D9 SYNOP, 12 stations, threshold 4.84 deg C fixed on 2022-2023, latest stored version of each station-time (D18; ordered by source insert time, then reference time, then our ingestion time, then payload hash). The 2025 labels do not depend on this rule for temperature because the 60 pairs of stored versions of 2025 do not differ in `t`; the rule is applied anyway. Labels are ground truth for scoring, never a model input and never a group of the interval. Unlabelled days count as not cold and are listed.

## 6. Decision rules, stated now
- Against RTE: the project target "lower MAE than RTE D-1" is written as reached by a candidate only if its 7-day interval against raw RTE lies entirely below 0. The raw RTE comparison is always shown next to the RTE* comparison, never alone, because raw RTE carries a systematic offset against consolidated data (D15). A claim to beat RTE* needs the interval against RTE* entirely below 0. If an interval contains 0 I write "not significant". If the 7-day and the 14-day verdicts disagree I write that the evidence is borderline.
- Candidate 1 against candidate 2: candidate 1 is called better only if its 7-day interval against candidate 2 lies entirely below 0. Otherwise I call them equivalent on the point forecast and note that candidate 2 is simpler. The choice of the model shown in the portfolio belongs to the developer.
- Interval calibration, same rules as EXP-012: seasonal calibration is called met only if the coverage of each of the four seasons is inside 78 to 82 percent (point values); overall calibration is called met only if the 7-day bootstrap interval of the overall coverage lies inside 78 to 82 percent. Otherwise I write whether the point value is inside and that the evidence is not enough to claim it.
- Cold days and holidays: the project contract target is a smaller error than RTE or a clearly explained gap. I report the gap and the days won. Any explanation I give is labelled a hypothesis unless a computed number shows it.
- Everything is published whatever it shows. No selection among candidates, windows, references or bootstrap settings after seeing 2025.
- Failures. A failure before any 2025 number is printed (the 2024 reproduction check, the database, MLflow) is fixed without changing any setting, the run is repeated, and the fact is written here. After 2025 numbers have been printed there is no rerun. If a defect in the analysis code is found afterwards, I publish the original result first, then the corrected one beside it, labelled as made after seeing 2025, and the original is never replaced. If only the MLflow logging fails, the numbers are already in `data/experiments/TEST-2025/results.json` and the run is logged from them.
- Test log: I record this run in my private test log (evaluation requirements (original contract section 6)).

## 7. Caveats stated now
One test year, one realisation. Candidate 1 and candidate 2 share the same experts, so their comparison is not independent evidence. Consolidated actuals are used for lags, losses and residuals, while production sees real-time values (open revision question). The 365-day seasonal pool holds one year of residuals of a model that was itself refitted on that year, and consecutive days are autocorrelated, so no coverage guarantee is claimed. RTE* uses the 2024 error table of RTE: if the relation between RTE D-1 and consolidated data changed after 2024, RTE* is a stale reference in 2025 and raw RTE is another distorted one; both are shown for that reason. Bridge and DST days are too few for any inference.

## 8. Expectations written before the run (hypotheses, no number computed)
- Both candidates beat raw RTE D-1 with an interval below 0, as in 2024, but a large part of that gap is the offset of raw RTE against consolidated data, so the comparison with RTE* is the informative one. I expect candidate 1 against RTE* to be small and not significant on the 7-day test, as in 2024. I know part of the 2025 RTE offset from my benchmark review, which is why I do not give a sign for RTE* in 2025.
- Candidate 1 has a lower MAE than candidate 2, by about the 57 MW seen in 2024 or less. It may not be significant.
- Cold days remain worse than RTE* for both candidates, because the GAM family under-forecasts cold days by about 900 MW in 2024. Holidays remain worse than RTE*.
- The overall coverage of the interval lies between 74 and 86 percent with a bootstrap interval too wide to claim calibration. At least one of the four seasons is outside 78 to 82 percent. Winter, whose pool in January and February 2025 contains only the winter 2024 residuals plus December 2024, is the season I trust least. Cold days stay far below 80 percent and holidays below 60 percent.
- MAE in 2025 is within 10 percent of the 2024 MAE of the same model. A larger drift would be a finding about the frozen models and not a reason to retune them.

## Reading of the results (the numbers above are the findings, this is my interpretation)
- Target "lower MAE than RTE D-1 over a full year": reached by both candidates on this test year against raw RTE (7-day intervals entirely below 0). Candidate 1 also beats RTE* significantly (-134 MW), which it did not in 2024 (-68 MW, not significant). Candidate 2 against RTE* is borderline (7-day interval touches 0, 14-day does not).
- RTE* is a weak reference in 2025. Its 2024 correction (about -1146 MW) over-corrects: the mean error of raw RTE in 2025 is -973 MW, so RTE* has a bias of +173 MW, and the monthly mean error of raw RTE ranges from -319 MW (January) to -1710 MW (May), which no annual hourly table can remove. The oracle correction with the 2025 table (post-hoc) leaves a smaller but still significant advantage for candidate 1 (-113 MW), so the gain over RTE does not come from the stale RTE* alone. Why RTE differs from consolidated data is still an open question and this test does not answer it.
- Candidate 1 is better than candidate 2 by about 60 MW, significant on both block lengths. They share experts, so this is not independent confirmation of the aggregation beyond what 2024 showed, but the sign and size repeat.
- Cold days and holidays: both candidates beat raw RTE on mean error, are worse than RTE* on cold days (1353 against 1235 for candidate 1) and about equal on holidays (1600 against 1566). The target "smaller error than RTE on cold days and holidays" is therefore met against raw RTE and not met against RTE*. With 31 and 11 days and a count of days won close to half, I do not claim more. Why candidate 1 loses on cold days against RTE* in the mean while winning 17 of 31 of them is not analysed here; the mean is probably driven by a few large winter misses (hypothesis, to be checked on the daily errors).
- Interval calibration. The overall point value (81.1 percent) is inside 78 to 82 but the 7-day interval [77.5, 84.9] is not inside it, so by the pre-registered rule overall calibration is not shown. By season, winter (82.7 percent) is just outside the band and the other three are inside, so seasonal calibration is not met by the rule, although the mean seasonal distance to 80 is 1.7 points. The per-season intervals are 12 to 19 points wide, so a point inside the band proves nothing. Coverage is not stable by month (November 59.6 percent, February 92.9 percent) nor by day type (cold 71 percent, holiday 42 percent): the interval is calibrated on average over a season and not on the days that matter most.

## Expectations of section 8 against the result
- Both candidates beat raw RTE significantly: confirmed.
- Candidate 1 against RTE* small and not significant: refuted (-134 MW, significant on both block lengths). I had said I could not give a sign for RTE* in 2025.
- Candidate 1 below candidate 2 by about 57 MW or less, maybe not significant: partly refuted (60.5 MW, significant).
- Cold days and holidays worse than RTE* for both candidates: cold days confirmed; holidays confirmed for candidate 2 (1739 against 1566) and for candidate 1 only by 34 MW on 11 days, which is within noise.
- Overall coverage between 74 and 86 percent: confirmed (81.1). At least one season outside 78 to 82: confirmed, but it is winter, over-covered (82.7), the season I said I trusted least, so I had the direction wrong. Cold days far below 80: partly confirmed (71 percent). Holidays below 60: confirmed (42 percent).
- 2025 MAE within 10 percent of 2024 for the same model: confirmed (-2.4 and -1.8 percent).
