# EXP-003: adding heating and cooling degrees cuts the 2024 MAE by 40 percent, but the model is still significantly worse than both RTE benchmarks
Reference: EXP-002 | Date: 2026-10-06
Single change: add two temperature features to the EXP-002 model, fixed a priori and never fitted (M4 option A): `hdd = max(0, 15 - T)` and `cdd = max(0, T - 20)`, with T the equal-weight mean of the 12 GFS points in degrees Celsius, taken from the run selected by D11 for each delivery day (primary 00 UTC of D-1, fallback 18 UTC of D-2), interpolated to 30 minutes with steps of that run only. Everything else is identical to EXP-002: one OLS per local Europe/Paris half-hour slot (48 models), calendar features (day of week with a holiday coded as Sunday, Fourier order 3, bridge, Christmas week), expanding window from 2021-03-23, weekly refit on Monday, walk-forward runner, no tuning. The bases 15 and 20 are assumptions stated before the run, not chosen on 2024.
Weather-missing rule (M11): weather-missing slots are left out of the fit (never imputed) and forecast by the EXP-002 calendar model, flagged by `backup_calendar`. In 2024 no slot used the backup.
Backtest period: 2024-01-01 to 2024-12-31 inclusive, Europe/Paris. Consumption read from a development build of the clean model `load_30min` for 2021-03-22 23:00 UTC to 2024-12-31 22:30 UTC. Weather read for delivery days 2021-03-24 to 2024-12-31 only (the reader refuses 2025 and the collector rows for 2025 and 2026 were not read). Nothing from 2025.

Coverage check before the run (`scripts/check_gfs_coverage.py` and the run itself): `raw.gfs_days` has 1379 distinct delivery days from 2021-03-24 to 2024-12-31 (1374 primary, 4 fallback, 1 weather missing: 2022-12-01, 48 slots without temperature, a training day only). No `not_collected` day, no selected run without TMP rows. The first training day 2021-03-23 predates the GFS archive and has no weather, so it drops out of the fit.

Conclusion: temperature is the missing information of EXP-002. The 2024 MAE falls from 2871 to 1719 MW, a significant gain of 1152 MW per day, and the cold-day error falls from 6982 to 2608 MW. The model is still significantly worse than raw RTE D-1 (by 354 MW) and than RTE* (by 822 MW), so the target of the project is not met yet and no claim of beating RTE is possible. Cold days remain 27 percent above raw RTE on the same days (2608 against 2062 MW).
Monthly diagnostic (my own check on the saved run, 2024 only, MAE of EXP-003 against RTE D-1, and mean error forecast minus actual): the model is better than raw RTE in May (1462 against 1660 MW), June (1147 against 1231) and July (887 against 1177), and worse in every other month, worst in January (3040 against 1906 MW) and February (2399 against 1589 MW). Its mean error is positive in winter (+1200 MW in January, +2243 in February) and close to zero in July, September and October (+587 MW in August). The weather features remove the seasonal shape error of EXP-002 (January 7212 to 3040 MW) but not the winter level offset. The cause of that offset is a hypothesis I did not test here: an expanding window starting in 2021 still averages the high consumption of 2021 and 2022 with the lower level after the 2022 energy crisis, and the model has no consumption lag to follow the level.
Limits: consolidated values replace the real-time vintages (protocol 2.2) and this model reads no consumption, so the revision leak reaches only its training targets. Training uses GFS forecasts (as in production), never observed temperature. The degree bases 15 and 20 are not tuned, so a better basis would likely lower the error; that is a later one-change experiment (M4 B or the GAM rung, EXP-005), not part of this one. The 2022-12-01 weather-missing day only touches the fit, and no 2024 slot used the calendar backup, so the M11 path is covered by unit tests, not by a real 2024 day.

`scripts/run_exp003.py --dry` runs everything except the MLflow logging and the file writes. The script stops if EXP-002 does not reproduce (MAE 2871.18) or if any delivery day has no collected TMP rows.
Leakage check: `tests/test_temperature_model.py` builds a synthetic archive and deletes every raw row published at or after the cutoff of day D (and the later days' selections), then requires identical weather features and identical `walk_forward` predictions for D (two tests, three days each, including a Monday refit and its following days). A mutant builder that reads the next day's temperature fails both tests.
