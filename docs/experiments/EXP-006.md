# EXP-006: a smoothed temperature with a 96 h half life does not measurably improve 2024 (907.7 against 911.9 MW, not significant)
Reference: EXP-005 | Date: 2026-10-06
Single change: add to the EXP-005 GAM a second penalized cubic B-spline (8 degrees of freedom) of T_s(t) = a * T_s(t-1) + (1 - a) * T(t), one step per half-hour slot. T is the D11 GFS national temperature. The chain runs over each delivery day's own selected run (published before that day's cutoff), never over a later download of an earlier day and never over an observation. A slot without temperature gets NaN and leaves the state unchanged (carried forward without decay). The state starts at the first temperature of the archive (2021-03-24). The GAM penalty stays 100.

Smoothing rule (D13). Provenance: the half life comes from the 2023-only selection script below, which can be rerun and reproduces the grid. The rule was not committed to Git before the 2024 run: the first commit containing this file (277e215) also contains the 2024 results, so the order "rule written, then 2024 run" cannot be proven from the repository history. My requirement to write the choice in EXP-006.md before the 2024 run was therefore not met in a verifiable way. This is a documentation gap, not evidence of tuning on 2024: the script deletes consumption after 2023-12-31 and reads weather to 2023-12-31 only. From EXP-007 on, the choice is committed on its own before any 2024 run.
- Chosen on 2023 only: the half life of T_s, from the grid 6, 12, 24, 48, 96, 192, 384 hours, by the lowest MAE of an inner walk-forward over 2023 (weekly refits from 2021-03-23, penalty 100, same features and runner). `scripts/select_smoothing_constant.py` deletes consumption after 2023-12-31 and reads weather to 2023-12-31 only.
- Result (17422 slots each, MAE in MW): 6 h: 1064.7; 12 h: 1057.4; 24 h: 1022.1; 48 h: 971.6; **96 h: 945.8**; 192 h: 981.0; 384 h: 1034.3. EXP-005 without the term, same period: 1057.0. The best value is inside the grid.
- Frozen: half life 96 h, a = 0.99640 per half-hour step, in `scripts/run_exp006.py` (`HALF_LIFE_HOURS`). Nothing else tuned.

EXP-006 is below EXP-005 in 7 months and above it in 5 (February, April, May, December, and November by 0.1 MW). The largest gain is January (-81 MW), the largest loss December (+104 MW). The mean error is smaller in absolute value in 7 months; the February error moves from -340 to +139 MW.

Conclusion: I cannot say the smoothed temperature helps on 2024. The mean daily MAE falls by 4 MW, the 7-day interval [-40, +24] contains 0, so I write "not significant". It also does not change the comparison with the benchmarks: still significantly better than raw RTE D-1 (-457 MW) and not significantly different from RTE* (+11 MW, interval contains 0). The 2023 inner walk-forward preferred it clearly (946 against 1057 MW, a 10 percent cut, same year the weight was chosen on, so optimistic), which shows how little a one-year choice transfers. Cold days, holidays and the RMSE are worse (descriptive, small samples), so I see no gain from the extra term on these days (not tested).
Not tested: why 2023 and 2024 disagree. A guess is that in 2023 the smoothed term absorbed the slow post-2022 fall in demand, which the lags now carry in 2024, but nothing here checks it. A half life of 96 h was the best of seven on 2023; 48 h and 192 h were within 40 MW of it, so the choice is not sharp.
Caveats that matter: (1) the revision leak of the lags is unchanged from EXP-004. (2) Inputs and all fitted numbers are the same as EXP-005 except the new term, but the penalty of 100 was chosen without that term. (3) The state starts at the first archive value, so the first weeks of 2021 depend on that choice; 2024 rows are far from it. (4) The model does not need the extra term to reach RTE* level, so I would not carry it to the milestone without a reason from another year.
