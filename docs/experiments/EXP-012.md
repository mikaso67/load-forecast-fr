# EXP-012: seasonal group (Mondrian) split-conformal intervals around the EXP-009 aggregate
Reference: EXP-010 (same point forecast, same order-statistic ranks, rolling pool across seasons) | Date: 2026-10-07
Single change: the calibration pool of the rolling split-conformal interval. EXP-010 pools the residuals of the last 182 local days whatever their season. EXP-012 pools, for a target day D in season s, the residuals of the same local slot on days of the same season s within the 365 days ending at D-2. The point forecast is unchanged. Developer decision, option A. Code: `seasonal_conformal_intervals` in `src/load_forecast/conformal.py`, tests `tests/test_conformal.py`.

# Pre-registration (written and committed before any seasonal interval was computed on project data)
Only unit tests on synthetic data were run (with a mutation check: removing the season filter makes four tests fail). No seasonal pool has been built from the residuals of the aggregate, on 2023 or on 2024. What I had already seen when I wrote this: the EXP-010 and EXP-011 results on 2024 (coverage by season and by month, cold days, holidays, widths), and my diagnostic finding that January and February 2023 were a high-error period for the tuned experts. They motivate the experiment. Nothing is tuned: there is no 2023 choice step, no grid and no dry run on 2024.

Point forecast: the EXP-009 shared-weight ML-Poly aggregate, 2023 burn-in then 2024, weights carried without reset. In the final run its 2024 MAE must be reproduced to 0.01 MW (828.52), the four experts to 0.01 MW (1015.70, 911.85, 885.32, 1113.94), and the EXP-010 intervals (N = 182, no season) slot by slot (maximum difference under 1e-6 MW against `data/experiments/EXP-010/intervals.csv`), all inside the same run.

Interval for delivery day D, local slot h (the same 48 slots as EXP-010):
- Residual r = actual minus point forecast, out of sample only (2023 burn-in residuals as in EXP-010, then 2024).
- Season of a day: winter December to February, spring March to May, summer June to August, autumn September to November, by the Europe/Paris local date (`evaluation.get_season`). The season is a calendar attribute, known in advance. The group is never defined by an observed quantity: the D9 cold label, the observed temperature and any other outcome are not used.
- Pool(D, h): the residuals of slot h on the local days d with D-2-364 <= d <= D-2 and season(d) = season(D). D-1 is never read (D2). A repeated autumn slot pools both occurrences, a spring slot that does not exist has none. Because the window is 365 days, the pool is one full season of the previous year (about 90 days) plus the days of the current season already known, and never mixes two seasons.
- With n finite residuals sorted, the lower bound is the order statistic of rank floor((n+1) * 0.1) and the upper bound the one of rank ceil((n+1) * 0.9), exactly as in EXP-010. A pool with a rank outside 1..n (n < 9) gives no interval and the slot is not scored. Interval = [point + lower, point + upper].
- 365 days is not tuned. It is the shortest round window that always contains one complete season of the previous year (a season has 90 to 92 days), which is what the developer's option A asks for. It is fixed here and never changed.
- Truncation test, written and run first (it failed at import before the function existed): `tests/test_conformal.py::test_seasonal_interval_of_day_d_ignores_actuals_after_d_minus_2` deletes every actual after D-2 and the interval of D must be identical, for nine days including the first days of seasons where D-1 and D-2 sit in another season. `test_a_seasonal_variant_that_absorbs_d_minus_1_fails_the_truncation_check` shows that the same check fails for a pool that ends at D-1. Other tests: the pool equals the same-season residuals of the window slot by slot, actuals of other seasons never move the interval, the pool at 1 March 2024 holds exactly the 92 days of spring 2023, a season with fewer than 9 pool days is undefined whatever the rest of the window holds, a window inside one season equals the plain rolling interval, DST days of 46 and 50 slots have an interval on every slot.

Coupled change and the control: restricting to a season needs a longer window, so "the calibration pool" changes in two coupled ways, the season filter and the window length (182 to 365 days). To avoid attributing to the season grouping an effect of the longer window, the same run also reports a control, EXP-010 with window 365 days and no season filter (`conformal_intervals`, window_days=365). The control is descriptive. It is not a candidate, nothing is selected from it, and no decision rule below depends on it.

What I will report for 2024 (full evaluation checklist from original contract section 6, nothing from 2025), EXP-012 side by side with EXP-010 (recomputed in the same run), EXP-011 (read from `data/experiments/EXP-011/intervals.csv`, its coverage checked against its stored results) and the 365-day control:
- Point forecast MAE, RMSE, MAPE against RTE D-1 and against RTE* a2 on common slots (unchanged from EXP-009, a reproduction check), the paired daily differences with 7-day block bootstrap intervals, and MAE by day type against both.
- Overall coverage of the 80 percent interval with a 95 percent moving block bootstrap interval (7-day blocks, 10000 draws, seed 20240; 14-day check), against 78 to 82 percent.
- Coverage and mean width by season, with a 95 percent block bootstrap interval for each season (descriptive: it resamples the whole calendar and keeps only the season's days, so the intervals are approximate), by day type (ordinary, cold with D9 labels, holiday, bridge, DST, the cold flag on all days; bridge and DST are descriptive, 2 days each), by month, share of days with every slot covered.
- Mean interval width in MW, pinball loss at 0.1 (lower bound), 0.5 (point forecast) and 0.9 (upper bound).
- Paired difference of the overall coverage, EXP-012 minus EXP-010, minus EXP-011 and minus the control, with a 7-day block bootstrap interval (14-day check), and the same difference by season (descriptive).
- Minimum pool size per group: the minimum, over the scored 2024 slots, of the number of residuals in the pool, overall, by season and by month, with the mean. January 2024: pool size, the share of its residuals that come from the 2023 burn-in (all of them, since no winter day of 2024 is known on 1 January), and the coverage and width of January 2024 against EXP-010.
- A single summary of seasonal calibration, descriptive: the mean absolute distance to 80 percent of the four seasonal coverages, for each of the four interval families.

Decision rules, stated now:
- By-season calibration is called met only if the coverage of each of the four seasons is inside 78 to 82 percent (point value, as in EXP-011); otherwise not met, whatever the overall figure. Overall calibration is called met only if the 7-day bootstrap interval of the overall coverage lies inside 78 to 82 percent; otherwise I write whether the point value is inside and that the evidence is not enough to claim it.
- EXP-012 is called a change of the overall coverage versus EXP-010 only if the paired interval of the difference excludes 0. A seasonal improvement is stated from the reduction of the mean absolute distance to 80 percent and from the per-season differences, with the remark that these intervals are descriptive. A wider interval that is not sharper is a cost I report through the width and the pinball losses, not an improvement.
- The 2024 run is made whatever the earlier numbers suggest, and a failure after numbers were printed is fixed without changing any setting, rerun and disclosed here.

Expectation written before the run, to be confirmed or refuted (a hypothesis, no number computed):
- Winter coverage rises clearly above 58.7 percent, because winter days are calibrated on winter residuals, which are larger than the residuals of the autumn and summer months that dominate the 182-day windows of EXP-010 in January. I do not expect it to reach 78 percent in every winter month: December 2024 and the cold days are driven by weather episodes, and the residual scale of winter 2023 (a high-error period, see my bias diagnostic) is not that of winter 2024. If January and February 2024 turn out too wide, that is the post-crisis regime of 2023 entering the pool and I will say so.
- Spring and summer coverage falls from 86.9 and 89.1 percent towards 80 to 85 percent, because their pools no longer contain the large winter residuals. Autumn stays within a few points of 79.3 percent.
- Overall coverage close to 78 to 82 percent with a bootstrap interval too wide to prove it. Mean width within about 10 percent of EXP-010, larger in winter and smaller in summer.
- Cold days and holidays improve at most modestly: cold days sit in winter, so some gain is expected, but a seasonal pool does not know the day type, so cold days stay well below 80 percent and holidays stay below 60 percent.
- The 365-day control behaves like EXP-010 by season (winter under-covered, spring and summer over-covered). If it does not, the longer window rather than the grouping is the lever.
- Caveats stated now: the 2023 residuals come from experts tuned on 2023 and from an aggregate whose weights start uniform on 1 January 2023, so the 2023 pools are probably optimistic and January 2024 is calibrated on them alone. Exchangeability inside a season pool is only approximate (residuals of consecutive days are autocorrelated and one year of history is used), so I claim no coverage guarantee. Consolidated actuals are used for residuals (open revision question in my private notes). One validation year, three winter months.

Expectation versus outcome (written before the run):
1. Winter coverage clearly above 58.7 percent, not necessarily 78 in every winter month: confirmed for the season (79.0). January (74.7) and December (71.4) are below 78, but February is 91.7 with a width of 3916 MW, so the winter pool is too wide in February. This fits the stated hypothesis that the high-error winter of 2023 enters the pool, but I did not test it.
2. Spring and summer fall towards 80 to 85: confirmed (83.6 and 81.0). Autumn within a few points of 79.3: confirmed (76.9).
3. Overall inside 78 to 82 with an interval too wide to prove it: confirmed (80.1, [76.1, 84.5]). Mean width within about 10 percent of EXP-010, larger in winter and smaller in summer: confirmed (+3.5 percent overall, winter 3931 against 2496, summer 1973 against 2592).
4. Cold days improve at most modestly and stay well below 80, holidays stay below 60: partly refuted. Cold days went from 40.3 to 63.4 percent (22 days, one winter), much more than modest, and are still well below 80. Holidays are 38.3 percent, below 60 and not improved.
5. The control behaves like EXP-010 by season: partly confirmed. It has winter under-covered (63.8) and summer over-covered (92.0), but spring (82.1) and autumn (87.2) differ from EXP-010 (86.9, 79.3). Its mean seasonal distance to 80 percent is 9.38 points against 9.53, so the longer window does not explain the gain: the season grouping does (2.19 points).
Conclusion: grouping the calibration pool by season moves the winter coverage from 58.7 to 79.0 percent (descriptive interval of the difference [+15.5, +26.0]) and brings spring and summer down from 87 and 89 to 84 and 81 percent, so the four seasonal coverages are on average 2.2 points from 80 instead of 9.5, and cold days improve from 40 to 63 percent. The cost is a wider winter interval (3931 against 2496 MW), a narrower summer one, and a mean width 3.5 percent larger overall; the pinball losses at 0.1 and 0.9 are lower by 10 and 19 MW against EXP-010 and by 4 and 9 MW against the control (descriptive, no test). The overall coverage is not detectably different from EXP-010. Seasonal calibration by the pre-registered rule is still not met, and holidays (38 percent) and two months (August 65.7, November 58.5) are as under-covered as before. The remaining misses are inside seasons (for example a cold onset in November), which a season label cannot see; this is a hypothesis, not tested.
Caveats: (1) one validation year, so about 91 days per season with strongly autocorrelated days; the seasonal intervals are about 20 points wide. (2) The 2023 residuals come from experts tuned on 2023 and from an aggregate whose weights start uniform on 1 January 2023, so January 2024 is calibrated on optimistic burn-in residuals (68 to 100 percent of its pool). (3) The February over-coverage (91.7 percent) and the wide winter interval are consistent with the 2023 regime entering the pool but not tested. (4) Residuals use consolidated actuals (open revision question in my private notes). (5) Exchangeability inside a season pool is approximate, so I claim no guarantee. (6) The 22 cold days are a small sample. (7) The control and the by-season paired differences are descriptive, a second family computed on 2024 as pre-registered.
