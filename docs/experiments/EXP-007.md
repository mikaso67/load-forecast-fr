# EXP-007: an online Kalman correction cuts the 2024 MAE from 912 to 885 MW, but the gain is not significant and cold days get worse
Reference: EXP-005 | Date: 2026-10-06
Single change: on top of the EXP-005 GAM (unchanged: same features, penalty 100, weekly Monday refit, expanding window from 2021-03-23), add a per-slot Kalman filter on the GAM residual (M7 option B). Code: `src/load_forecast/kalman.py`, tests `tests/test_kalman.py`.

# Pre-registration (written and committed before any run on 2023 or 2024 data of this model)
Only a 3-week smoke run on June 2022 (outside both evaluation years) was done to check that the code runs and how long it takes.

Model, per Paris local half-hour slot, one time step per delivery day:
- Observation: y_t - gam_t = delta_t' x_t + e_t, with e_t ~ N(0, noise). The state follows delta_t = delta_(t-1) + w_t, w_t ~ N(0, diag(q)). delta_0 = 0, so the forecast is exactly the GAM before any update; the prior covariance is 10000 times the identity (a diffuse prior of 100 MW).
- Regressors x_t = (1, z_base, z_lags, z_smooth). The three z are the GAM partial effects of that slot (`SlotGAM.partial_effects`): `base` = constant, calendar and special-day terms; `lags` = lag_d2, lag_d7, lag_last and their three special-day indicators; `smooth` = the temperature spline (tails included). Each is standardized with the mean and sd over the training days of the refit, so every state is a correction in MW.
- Fitted parameters: the noise variance and the four diagonal entries of Q, per slot, by Gaussian maximum likelihood (L-BFGS-B on log parameters, 100 iterations at most, warm start from the previous fit) on the training days of a refit, with the GAM of that refit. Re-estimated at the first weekly refit of each calendar quarter, on the days whose consumption is known at that refit cutoff and not later than the Saturday before it (D-2 rule), then held fixed to the next quarter. Nothing else is fitted or tuned: prior covariance, bounds, iteration limit, quarterly schedule and the choice of regressors are fixed here.
- Timing (checklist item 7): the forecast of day D uses the filter updated with the full day D-2 and nothing later. Consumption comes only from the history known at the cutoff of D.
- Missing rule: a day with a missing lag or no weather has NaN GAM features, gets no update on that day and takes the EXP-005 forecast (flagged `backup_exp005`, as in EXP-006).
- Caveat written in advance: the GAM residuals on the training window are in-sample, so the fitted noise variance is probably smaller than the out-of-sample one and the filter probably reacts faster than it should.

Choice on 2023 only (D13), two options, one rule:
- `restart`: after each weekly GAM refit the filter is rerun from the start of the window on the new GAM effects.
- `keep`: after a refit the state and covariance carry over, the new effects are used from the refit on.
- `scripts/select_kalman_mode.py` runs both over 2023 (consumption deleted after 2023-12-31, weather read to 2023-12-31, same GAM fits, same variances) and prints the MAE of the plain GAM, `restart` and `keep`.
- Rule: choose `keep` only if its 2023 MAE is lower than that of `restart` by more than 0.5 MW, otherwise `restart` (stateless, simpler to deploy). The 2024 run is made whatever the 2023 result is, including when the filter does not beat the plain GAM in 2023.

# 2023 choice (recorded in its own commit, before any 2024 run)
`scripts/select_kalman_mode.py` was run once on 2023 (about 355 s; the pre-registration commit is f47f42f). Same 17422 slots for each row, MAE in MW:

| 2023 inner walk-forward | MAE (MW) |
|-------------------------|----------|
| plain GAM (EXP-005 model, reproduces the 1057.0 of the EXP-005 selection) | 1057.00 |
| Kalman, `restart` | 863.98 |
| Kalman, `keep` | 865.59 |

`keep` minus `restart` = +1.60 MW. By the rule, `keep` is chosen only if it is lower by more than 0.5 MW, so the choice is **`restart`**. The difference of 1.6 MW between the two options is small; the gap to the plain GAM (about 190 MW) is not. 2023 is the year the choice was made on, so the gain is optimistic.
Fitted variances (median over the 48 slots, in MW per day, standard deviation of the drift of the intercept, base, lags and smooth states): the intercept drifts by 150 to 190 MW per day, the lags state by 5 to 240 MW per day (5.5 in the first fit, 140 to 240 in the four others), the base state by 20 to 110 MW per day and the smooth state by 4 to 90 MW per day; noise sd 820 to 900 MW. These are large drifts, so the filter forgets quickly (an effective memory of a few days), and the first fit differs a lot from the later ones, a sign that the likelihood is flat in these directions. Every one of the 5 quarterly fits stopped at the 100-iteration limit fixed in advance without meeting the tolerance, so the variances are good but not exactly converged maximum likelihood values; I did not change the limit after seeing this.

EXP-007 is below EXP-005 in 8 months and above it in 4 (February +115, May +99, September +7, December +126 MW). The largest gains are August (-191), July (-126), April (-109) and October (-107). The mean error is smaller in absolute value in 8 months and larger in 4 (March, April, August, December).

Filter diagnostics (2024, quarterly maximum-likelihood fits, median over the 48 slots): noise sd 836 to 873 MW; drift sd per day of the intercept 141 to 147 MW, base state 60 to 66 MW, lags state 186 to 215 MW, smooth state 72 to 95 MW. Three of the four fits stopped at the 100-iteration limit, one converged. The filter moves the forecast by a mean absolute 594 MW (mean +21 MW) away from the GAM, on a model whose MAE is 912 MW, so it is an active correction, not a small one. The pre-registered caveat shows in the numbers: the in-sample noise sd (about 850 MW) is well below the 2024 out-of-sample RMSE of the GAM (1290 MW); I did not test whether this is why the filter reacts so strongly.

Conclusion: I cannot say the Kalman correction helps on 2024. The mean daily MAE falls by 27 MW (3 percent) but the 7-day interval [-86, +25] contains 0, so I write "not significant"; the RMSE is unchanged (1294 against 1290 MW). It stays significantly better than raw RTE D-1 (-479 MW) and not significantly different from RTE* (-11 MW). The gain seen on 2023, the year of the choice (864 against 1057 MW), shrinks to a seventh in 2024 (885 against 912 MW): the same pattern as EXP-006, a one-year result that does not transfer. The correction trades days: it helps ordinary days and holidays and hurts cold days by 322 MW, which is what an aggressive filter would do when residuals of cold days are large and short-lived (a guess, not tested).
Not tested: why 2023 and 2024 differ; whether a smaller drift (variances not fitted in-sample) would keep the gain without the cold-day loss; the intercept-only filter (M7 option C); updating with the morning of D-1.
Caveats that matter: (1) the revision leak of the lags is unchanged from EXP-004, and the filter reads the same consolidated values (open question in my private notes). (2) The variances come from in-sample residuals and 3 of 4 fits hit the iteration limit. (3) The `restart` and `keep` options differed by 1.6 MW on 2023, so the choice matters little. (4) I would not carry this to the milestone without a reason from another year.

Leakage check: `tests/test_kalman.py` perturbs the consumption of the early slots of D-1 (all but the lag_last slot, which the features read legitimately) and requires identical forecasts in both modes; the same test with `UPDATE_LAG_DAYS` set to 1 (a D-1 update) produces different forecasts and is detected; perturbing D-2 does change the correction, so the filter really uses it; truncating the history at the cutoff leaves the forecast unchanged. The textbook scalar filter reproduces the batched log-likelihood, and without drift and with a diffuse prior the filter equals least squares. The variances use only days up to the Saturday before the refit Monday. Standardization uses training days only.
