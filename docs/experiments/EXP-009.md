# EXP-009: ML-Poly aggregation of four experts beats the best single expert on 2024 (829 against 885 MW)
Reference: EXP-007 (best single expert) | Date: 2026-10-07
Single change: replace the single forecast by an online convex combination of four frozen experts (modeling plan rung 5, M8 option A). Code: `src/load_forecast/aggregation.py`, tests `tests/test_aggregation.py`.

# Pre-registration (written and committed before any run of the aggregation on 2023 or 2024 data)
Only unit tests on synthetic data were run. Nothing of this aggregation has been scored on project data.

Experts, all with the frozen configuration of their own experiment, backups as in their runs:
- EXP-004 linear per slot with lags (calendar backup), EXP-005 GAM (smoothing weight 100, EXP-004 backup), EXP-007 GAM plus restart Kalman (EXP-005 backup), EXP-008 LightGBM (lr 0.1, 15 leaves, min_data 100, 6400 rounds, EXP-005 backup). After the backup chain every expert has a forecast for every slot.
- Burn-in: out-of-sample 2023 forecasts of the four experts, weekly refits from 2021-03-23, consumption deleted after 2023-12-31 and weather read to 2023-12-31 (`scripts/select_aggregation.py`). No saved 2023 prediction file exists for any expert, so they are computed once and cached outside git. 2024 forecasts are recomputed in the final run and must reproduce the MAE of EXP-004 (1015.70), EXP-005 (911.85), EXP-007 (885.32) and EXP-008 (1113.94) to 0.01 MW.
- Caveat on the burn-in: the smoothing weight, the Kalman mode and the LightGBM configuration were chosen on 2023, so the experts are optimistic on 2023, LightGBM probably the most (grid winner on the grid edge). The weights carried into 2024 are therefore a little biased towards whatever was tuned hardest. Not corrected, only reported.

Aggregator (nothing is tuned):
- ML-Poly, Gaillard, Stoltz and van Erven (2014), gradient trick. Weight of expert k proportional to eta_k times the positive part of its cumulative regret R_k, with eta_k = 1 / (1 + sum of squared instantaneous regrets). Uniform weights while no regret is positive, hence on 2023-01-01. Losses in MW.
- Loss: absolute error (the scored metric). Instantaneous regret of expert k on a slot: sign(p - y) times (p - f_k), p being the aggregate actually issued for that slot.
- Information rule: the weights used for day D are a function of the experts' forecasts and of the actuals of full local days up to D-2 only. Day D-1 is never absorbed (checklist item 7). The weights of day d used in the regret are the ones issued for d.
- Truncation test (`tests/test_aggregation.py`): the aggregate for D is identical when every actual after D-2 is deleted, and a variant that absorbs D-1 fails the same test.
- Weights carry from 2023 into 2024 without reset. Missing actual slots add no regret. A round is one local day; an autumn repeated slot adds both occurrences into the same round of that slot.
- The one unavoidable choice: weights per local half-hour slot (48 weight vectors, 365 rounds a year each, the plan in the task) or one shared weight vector (one round a day, the regrets of the 48 slots added up). Rule, fixed here: run both on 2023 alone (weights uniform on 2023-01-01), compare the MAE over all 2023 slots with an actual value; choose the lower one, and choose `shared` if the two are within 0.5 MW (fewer weights to estimate). Both variants and the best single expert on 2023 are printed for context.
- The 2024 run is made whatever the 2023 result, including when the aggregate does not beat EXP-007 in 2023.

What I will report for 2024 (full evaluation checklist from original contract section 6, nothing from 2025):
- MAE, RMSE, MAPE of EXP-009, the four experts, raw RTE D-1 and RTE* a2, on common slots.
- MAE by day type (ordinary, cold, holiday, bridge, DST) against RTE and RTE*, slot-weighted.
- Paired daily-MAE differences with the 7-day block bootstrap (14-day as a check, 10000 draws, seed 20240) against EXP-007, EXP-005, raw RTE D-1 and RTE* a2. If an interval contains 0, "not significant".
- Correlation matrix of the daily mean signed errors and of the daily MAE between the four experts (2024).

- Not reported: interval coverage (point forecast, M9 comes next).

Conclusion: the aggregate improves the best single expert significantly (-57 MW, 6 percent) and raw RTE D-1 (-536 MW), and its point MAE is 68 MW under RTE*, but that last gap is not significant with the 7-day block (the 14-day check is). The low correlation of LightGBM with the GAM family and the online reweighting are the likely sources of the gain (a hypothesis: no ablation was run). Cold days and holidays remain worse than RTE*.
Caveats: (1) the 2023 burn-in experts were tuned on 2023, so the carried weights may favour the tuned models; (2) the choice of shared weights rests on a 9 MW gap on one year; (3) the weights are updated on consolidated actuals, the open revision question in my private notes applies; (4) the cold-day weight of LightGBM is only 0.28 although it was unbiased on cold days in EXP-008; this run does not explain why.
