# EXP-002: a per-slot linear model on calendar features alone does not significantly beat the seasonal naive

Canonical MLflow run for the unchanged calendar model and D15 overall scores:
`16cd1454f8fa42178a5575ba360d6200` (dual-benchmark rerun).
The original run `7d94654e347f460a80b99ceb51359244` is superseded for these

Reference: EXP-001 | Date: 2026-10-05
Single change: replace the seasonal naive forecast by one ordinary least squares model per local Europe/Paris half-hour slot (48 models, M5), trained on calendar features only: day of week with a holiday coded as Sunday (M2 A), Fourier terms of day of year (order 3), bridge indicator, Christmas week indicator (Dec 24 to 31). No temperature, no lag, no consumption input at all. Expanding window from 2021-03-23, weekly refit on Monday (D5, D6), run with the walk-forward runner. No tuning.
Backtest period: 2024-01-01 to 2024-12-31 inclusive, Europe/Paris. Data read: 2021-03-22 23:00 UTC to 2024-12-31 22:30 UTC from a development build of the clean model `load_30min`; nothing from 2025.

Conclusion: calendar features alone reduce overall MAE by about 378 MW compared with the seasonal naive, but the daily difference is not significant. The model is significantly worse than RTE D-1. This is the expected floor of the ladder: it has no weather and no level information.
Monthly diagnostic (my own check on the saved predictions, 2024 only): the model error is worst in winter (MAE 7212 MW in January, 5593 in February, 4255 in December) and at or below RTE in May, June, July and September (MAE 1653, 794, 816 and 966 MW versus RTE 1660, 1231, 1177 and 1200 MW), but above it in August (1556 versus 869 MW). The mean error (forecast minus actual) is +1104 MW over the year and +5380 MW in February. A calendar-only model cannot see a mild or a cold winter, and its level comes from an expanding window refitted every Monday, so it starts from the 2021 to 2023 average, which includes the 2022 crisis year, and only slowly absorbs 2024 targets. I did not isolate the share of each cause, so I draw no conclusion on it.
Data caveat for the whole project, found while checking the bias: RTE D-1 forecast minus the consolidated consumption has a mean of -354 MW (2021), -574 (2022), -1131 (2023) and -1146 MW (2024; every 2024 month between -558 and -1602 MW). With an MAE of 1365 MW in 2024, most of the RTE error is a systematic negative mean error. I did not find the cause (a definition difference between the consolidated consumption and RTE's forecast scope is a hypothesis, not a result). Any model trained on the consolidated consumption learns this offset for free, so beating RTE on MAE may partly be a definition effect. This needs a decision from the lead before rungs 2 and above are interpreted.

## D15 dual benchmark addendum (2026-10-06)

forecasts use the same 17,566 slots (two excluded under D12).

The mean daily MAE difference, EXP-002 minus RTE*, is +1974.35 MW;
the 7-day block 95% interval is [1313.44, 2432.38] MW and the 14-day check is

difference and remains not significant.

availability caveat in `docs/rte_benchmark.md` section 5.1. No alternate window,
model change, or 2025 value was used. This addendum adds overall dual scoring;
