# EXP-004: adding three consumption lags cuts the 2024 MAE from 1719 to 1016 MW and beats raw RTE D-1, but stays significantly worse than the leak-free RTE* and rests on consolidated lags
Reference: EXP-003 | Date: 2026-10-06
Single change: add lagged consumption to the EXP-003 features (M3 option A): `lag_d2` and `lag_d7` (the same Paris wall-clock half-hour two and seven calendar days earlier) and `lag_last` (the D-1 09:30 local slot, the last slot that ends two hours before the cutoff, D2), plus one indicator per lag day (`special_d2`, `special_d7`, `special_last`: holiday or bridge on that day, M3 caveat). Everything else is identical to EXP-003: 48 OLS models per local slot, calendar features, hdd and cdd with fixed bases 15 and 20 from the D11 GFS run, expanding window from 2021-03-23, weekly Monday refit, walk-forward runner, no tuning.
Lag matching: the local wall-clock time is shifted by whole days and converted to UTC, never by row offset (M1). A wall-clock time that does not exist (the 02:xx hour of a spring change) gives a missing lag. A repeated wall-clock time (autumn change) resolves to the second, standard-time occurrence, the one the source keeps (D12).
Missing-lag rule (`src/load_forecast/lag_model.py`, reusable in production): if any lag of a target day is missing, the whole day carries no lag features. It then drops out of the fit and its forecast is the EXP-003 forecast, flagged `backup_exp003`. In 2024 this happened on 2 days, 96 slots: 2024-04-02 and 2024-04-07, the days whose D-2 and D-7 lag day is the 23-hour spring change day 2024-03-31. No lag was missing for any other reason (no data gap).
Backtest period: 2024-01-01 to 2024-12-31 inclusive, Europe/Paris. Consumption from a development build of the clean model `load_30min` up to 2024-12-31 22:30 UTC (the loader stops at END), weather for delivery days 2021-03-24 to 2024-12-31 (coverage check of EXP-003 unchanged: no `not_collected` day). Nothing from 2025.

The winter over-forecast of EXP-003 (+1200 MW in January, +2243 in February) is gone: the mean error is within 280 MW of zero in every month. The model beats raw RTE in every month except August (1005 against 869 MW) and December (1737 against 1588 MW).
Sensitivity diagnostic (not used for any choice): an OLS fit of the same features on 2021-03 to 2024-06, one model per slot, gives lag coefficients that average 0.16 (D-2), 0.06 (D-7) and 0.35 (last value), a sum of 0.57. A level offset d in the lags therefore moves the forecast by about 0.57 d.

Conclusion: lags are the level information EXP-003 was missing. The 2024 MAE falls by 703 MW per day (a 41 percent cut), significantly, the winter bias disappears, and the model is now significantly better than raw RTE D-1 (-349 MW per day); by day type it is below raw RTE for ordinary, cold and holiday days and above it for the bridge and DST rows (2 days each, descriptive). Against the leak-free corrected benchmark RTE* the model is still significantly worse (+119 MW per day, intervals [+28, +204] and [+18, +182]), so I cannot claim to beat RTE for the target "lower MAE than RTE D-1" without the caveat below. Cold days: 1945 MW against 2062 MW for raw RTE and 1489 MW for RTE*.
Caveat that matters most (revision leak, now live): a lag is a consumption input, and in the backtest it is a consolidated value, while in production it will be a real-time value, and the two may differ by about a gigawatt: raw RTE D-1 sits about 1146 MW below consolidated consumption in 2024 but about 130 MW above real-time consumption since 2026-07 (docs/rte_benchmark.md; different periods, cause not proven, so the gap is a hypothesis, not a measurement). The EXP-003 note said the revision leak reached only the training targets; with lags it reaches the inputs. If production lags sit d below the consolidated ones, the forecast shifts by about 0.57 d. This is also why the raw-RTE comparison is the weaker one: part of the apparent gain over raw RTE is the same consolidation offset that D15 corrects in RTE*. The comparison with RTE* is the fair one, and it is not yet won. The size of the real-time versus consolidated gap on consumption must be measured in production before any claim.
Limits: lag coefficients are one slope per slot for every weekday, so a lag from a Saturday or Sunday (for example D-2 for a Monday) is treated like a weekday lag, except for holidays and bridges; a day-of-week interaction or the GAM rung may fix part of the remaining gap to RTE*, but that is a hypothesis I did not test. The special-day indicators rest on few events (holiday or bridge on the lag day, tens of days). The degree bases are still not tuned. Two days (2024-04-02, 2024-04-07) use the EXP-003 forecast because a lag day was the spring change day: this is a design choice (never a neighbouring slot), not a data gap.

`scripts/run_exp004.py --dry` runs everything except the MLflow logging and the file writes. The script stops if EXP-003 does not reproduce (MAE 1719.06) or if any delivery day has no collected TMP rows.
Leakage check: `tests/test_lag_model.py` hands the lag builder the full history and then the history with every value after the last usable slot deleted (D2, no `published_at`), for three days including a spring and an autumn neighbourhood, and requires identical features. Two mutants fail the same check: a builder reading the same slot of D-1 and a builder reading the latest value it receives. A runner-level test deletes every later value and compares `walk_forward` predictions for three days (a Monday refit, the next day and a later midweek day). The runner itself already truncates the history (`known_history`), so the direct builder test is the one that would catch a builder reading data it should not.
