# EXP-010: rolling split-conformal 80% intervals around the EXP-009 aggregate
Reference: EXP-009 (same point forecast, no interval) | Date: 2026-10-07
Single change: add a rolling split-conformal 80% interval to the EXP-009 point forecast (modeling plan M9 option A, protocol 4.1). The point forecast is not changed. Code: `src/load_forecast/conformal.py`, tests `tests/test_conformal.py`.

# Pre-registration (written and committed before any interval was computed on project data)
Only unit tests on synthetic data were run. No residual of the aggregate has been looked at.

Point forecast: the EXP-009 shared-weight ML-Poly aggregate, 2023 burn-in then 2024, weights carried without reset. Its 2024 MAE must be reproduced to 0.01 MW (828.52) in the final run, and the four experts to 0.01 MW (1015.70, 911.85, 885.32, 1113.94), by recomputing them as in `scripts/run_exp009.py`.

Interval for delivery day D, local slot s:
- Residuals r = actual minus point forecast, out of sample only: 2023 residuals come from the burn-in aggregate (experts refitted weekly without any later data, weights updated with actuals up to D-2), 2024 residuals from the 2024 run.
- Calibration set: the residuals of slot s over the N local days D-2-N+1 to D-2 (last full day known at the cutoff, D2; D-1 is never read). A repeated autumn slot pools both occurrences, a spring slot that does not exist has none.
- With n residuals sorted, the lower bound is the order statistic of rank floor((n+1) * 0.1) and the upper bound the one of rank ceil((n+1) * 0.9). Under exchangeability each tail misses with probability at most 0.1, so coverage is at least 80 percent. If a rank falls outside 1..n the interval is undefined and the slot is not scored (does not happen with N >= 28).
- Interval = [point + lower, point + upper]. Nothing is fitted, no parameter other than N.
- Truncation test: `tests/test_conformal.py::test_interval_of_day_d_ignores_actuals_after_d_minus_2` deletes every actual after D-2 and the interval of D must be identical; `test_a_variant_that_absorbs_d_minus_1_fails_the_truncation_check` shows the same check fails when D-1 is absorbed.

Choice of N, on 2023 only, `scripts/select_conformal_window.py`:
- Grid fixed here: N in {28, 56, 91, 182} days.
- Data: the cached 2023 expert forecasts (`data/experiments/EXP-009/experts_2023.csv`), aggregated as in EXP-009 (shared variant), residuals against the 2023 consolidated actuals.
- Scored slots: the same for every N, the slots of the delivery days whose longest window (182 days) is full, that is from 2023-07-03 (first residual day 2023-01-01, plus 181 further days to fill the window, plus 2 days of lag) to 2023-12-31. The first half of 2023 cannot be used to compare N because the longest window has no data there. The 2023 expert forecasts are optimistic (hyperparameters were tuned on 2023, see EXP-009), so the 2023 residuals are probably a little smaller than future ones, which pushes towards narrower intervals and lower coverage on 2024.
- Rule: the N whose coverage is closest to 80 percent. If several N are within 0.5 percentage point of the best distance, the one with the smallest mean width. Printed for context only: block bootstrap interval of the coverage, and coverage and width over every day where each N is defined.
- The choice is recorded in this file in its own commit before any 2024 run. `scripts/run_exp010.py` refuses to start unless that commit is in the history and names the window used.
- The 2024 run is made whatever the 2023 result, including when the coverage is far from 80 percent.

What I will report for 2024 (full evaluation checklist from original contract section 6, nothing from 2025):
- Point forecast MAE, RMSE, MAPE against RTE D-1 on common slots (unchanged from EXP-009, a reproduction check) and MAE by day type.
- Overall coverage of the 80% interval with a 95% moving block bootstrap interval (7-day blocks, 10000 draws, seed 20240; 14-day block as a check), against the 78 to 82 percent target. If the bootstrap interval is not inside 78 to 82, I write whether the point value is inside and that the evidence is not enough to claim calibration.
- Coverage and mean width by season (winter, spring, summer, autumn) and by day type (ordinary, cold with D9 labels, holiday, bridge, DST, plus the cold flag on all days).
- Mean interval width in MW, pinball loss at 0.1 (lower bound), 0.5 (point forecast) and 0.9 (upper bound).
- Coverage by month and the share of days with all slots covered, as diagnostics of drift (descriptive).
- RTE D-1 has no interval, so there is nothing to compare the interval with. Not reported: anything on 2025.

# 2023 choice (recorded in its own commit, before any 2024 run)
`scripts/select_conformal_window.py` was run once on 2023 after the pre-registration commit 6464949 (log `data/select_conformal_window.log`). Scored slots: 8736 slots, 182 days, 2023-07-03 to 2023-12-31, identical for every N. Coverage of the 80% interval, 95% 7-day block bootstrap interval in brackets, and mean width:

| N (days) | coverage | mean width (MW) | distance to 80% |
|----------|----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| 28 | 81.3% [76.4, 86.3] | 2597 | 1.3 points |
| 56 | 73.7% [67.5, 80.2] | 2132 | 6.3 points |
| 91 | 73.2% [66.3, 80.5] | 2003 | 6.8 points |
| 182 | 78.3% [71.0, 86.0] | 2333 | 1.7 points |

By the rule, N=28 is the closest (1.3 points) and N=182 is within 0.5 point of it (1.7 points), so the two are tied and the narrower one wins: the chosen window is **N=182**. For context, over every day where each window is defined (more days for the shorter windows), coverage is 84.3, 79.8, 79.7 and 82.6 percent for N = 28, 56, 91 and 182.

Reading: the coverages differ by up to 8 points but every bootstrap interval is 9 to 13 points wide, so the 2023 evidence cannot separate N=28 from N=182 and the tie rule decided. The dips at 56 and 91 days are unexplained (not investigated). The scored half year is July to December only.

Difference vs reference: the point forecast is the reference, unchanged (same MAE to 0.01 MW). The interval has no reference experiment to compare with (RTE D-1 has no interval), so no paired test applies.
Conclusion: the observed overall coverage of the rolling conformal interval is inside the target (78.5%, interval [73.9, 83.9], which is not inside 78 to 82) but it is not calibrated by season or on the days that matter: it covers only 59% in winter, 40% on cold days and 42% on holidays, and it is too wide (87 to 89%) in spring and summer. The pattern, wide after the warm season and narrow when the cold season starts, fits a 182-day window that carries the error scale of the previous season; this is a hypothesis, no other window was run on 2024 and none will be tuned on it. The 2023 choice (N=182 against N=28, tied within 0.5 point) was weak evidence, as the 2023 bootstrap intervals already showed.
Caveats: (1) the 2023 residuals come from experts tuned on 2023, so they are probably a little small; (2) the residuals are computed against consolidated actuals, the open revision question in my private notes applies; (3) a single year, and winter 2024 is 91 days with strong autocorrelation, so the winter figure has a wide interval (not computed per season); (4) the width is the same for all weekdays and day types within a slot, so holidays and cold days, whose errors are larger, are not covered by design of the method.
Next one-change experiments, not run here: a shorter window (N=28 was the other candidate on 2023), adaptive conformal inference (Gibbs and Candes, 2021, named in M9), or residuals pooled by day type or season.
