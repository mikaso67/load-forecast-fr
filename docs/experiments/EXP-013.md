# EXP-013: neither the share of France on school holiday nor forecast solar radiation significantly reduces the 2024 error of the GAM or the LightGBM expert
Reference: EXP-005 (GAM expert) and EXP-008 (LightGBM expert) | Date: 2026-10-16
Single change: add one input to each expert, the population share of mainland France whose school zone is on holiday on the delivery day. Everything else is frozen: features, GAM smoothing weight 100, LightGBM configuration `lr=0.1, leaves=15, min_data=100, rounds=6400`, weekly Monday refit, expanding window from 2021-03-23, missing-lag and missing-weather backups. Code: `src/load_forecast/school_holidays.py`, tests `tests/test_school_holidays.py`, script `scripts/run_exp013.py`.

Scope: this file covers the school holiday input (part b), then the solar radiation input (part a) and the combination of both, each with its own pre-registration and results below.

- Population: INSEE legal populations 2021 (in force from 2024-01-01), archive https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/fichier/7739582/ensemble.zip downloaded on 2026-10-16 14:46 UTC, stored as `data/external/insee_population/pop_legales_2021.zip` (1,126,056 bytes, SHA256 `f002a648fbb5d0fbf4f2f528997cd4ce3ebee3b5366ecdb55d16107dd4043816`); I use its member `donnees_departements.csv` (SHA256 `0515ca6b83370eb50cef2067b69ab6eb9b75b1af4f52506ef53123d98bb68d73`), column `PMUN` (municipal population).

## Construction
- Rows kept: zones A, B and C (mainland without Corsica, the perimeter of the RTE national load), pupils' rows (`population` is `-` or `Élèves`; the summer has a shorter teachers' row which I drop), school years 2020-2021 to 2024-2025 (older years still list Caen and Rouen; later years are drafts with some empty periods).
- Dates: `start_date` and `end_date` are Paris local midnights (checked: the code refuses any other time). A day is on holiday when `start <= day < end`, with `end` the day school resumes. Weekends inside a holiday period count as holiday days; weekends outside count as 0. Every listed period counts, including the Ascension bridge (Thursday to Sunday, already partly coded as a public holiday and a bridge day).
- Weights: share of the zone in the 2021 municipal population of the 94 departments. Zone A 26.07%, zone B 45.79%, zone C 28.15%. The share of a day is the sum of the weights of the zones on holiday, so it takes the values 0, 0.261, 0.281, 0.458, 0.542, 0.719, 0.739 and 1. Over 2021-03-23 to 2024-12-31 it is 0 on 813 days and 1 on 398 days (summer, Christmas, Toussaint and the Ascension bridge are common to all zones).
- One population vintage for all years: the zone shares move by a fraction of a point between vintages, so I accept that the 2021 weights are applied to 2021 to 2023 training days as well (they were published in December 2023). The calendar itself is fixed years ahead by decree, so the value for a delivery day is known before its 12:00 D-1 cutoff and never reads consumption.

## Choice made on 2023 only (D13)
One encoding choice, between two candidates:
- `all_days`: the share on every day;
- `working_days`: the share on Monday to Friday outside public holidays, 0 otherwise (school is closed on those days anyway, so the input only marks the working days on which children are at home).

Rule: run the inner walk-forward of `scripts/select_gam_smoothing.py` on 2023 (consumption deleted after 2023-12-31, weather read to 2023-12-31, refits from 2021-03-23) with the EXP-005 GAM plus each candidate, and the EXP-005 GAM without the input (it must reproduce 1057.0 MW). Choose the candidate with the lowest 2023 MAE; within 1.0 MW of each other, take `all_days` (the simpler one). The chosen encoding is used for both experts: I do not run the 2023 LightGBM walk-forward twice (about 45 minutes each), and the encoding is a property of the input, not of the model. The 2024 runs are made whatever the 2023 result, including when the input does not help in 2023.

# Part (a) solar radiation and (a)+(b): pre-registration (committed before any run of the solar input on 2023 or 2024)

## Data
- Source: GFS 0.25 degree `DSWRF:surface` (downward short-wave radiation flux, W m-2), same table `raw.gfs_points`, same 12 points, same selected D11 run per delivery day (`raw.gfs_days`) and same steps f021 to f047 as the temperature. The history was backfilled for 2021 to 2024. Checked on the database before writing this section: every one of the 1,378 delivery days with a selected run up to 2024-12-31 has its 324 DSWRF rows (27 steps times 12 points), no negative value, no duplicate.
- Days without weather: 2021-03-23 and 2022-12-01 have no GFS run at all (neither primary nor fallback, status `weather missing`). The existing rule applies unchanged: the feature is NaN, the day is dropped from training, and a forecast day would use the backup of the reference chain. 2021-03-23 is also before the first weather day used by EXP-003 to EXP-013 (2021-03-24). Neither day is in 2023 or 2024.
- Cutoff: the code refuses any radiation row whose file or index was published at or after 12:00 Paris on D-1, as for temperature.

## Construction (`src/load_forecast/solar_features.py`)
- GFS stores DSWRF as the mean flux since the last 6-hour reset. The mean flux of the hour ending at step t is `k A_k - (k - 1) A_{k-1}` with `k = t - reset` (rule in `docs/weather_sources.md`). The first step of a run (f021, third hour of its window) cannot be differenced and is not used. Negative values from rounding are set to 0.
- Each hourly mean is stamped at the middle of its hour, then interpolated linearly to the half-hour grid inside the same run (no step of another run is mixed), then averaged over the 12 points with equal weights, exactly as `temperature_c`. The column is `solar_wm2`. A missing step or point gives NaN, never 0.
- Check on the real data (2021-03-24 to 2024-12-31): zero at night, June 2024 mean peaking at 669 W m-2 at 13:00 Paris, monthly means of 2023 from 44 (December) to 293 (June) W m-2.

## How the input enters each expert
- GAM (EXP-005): one more linear term per slot model. A slot where the input is constant (night) drops the term automatically, as any constant linear column. I do not use a smooth term: at a given slot the effect of radiation is mainly embedded solar generation and lighting, both close to linear in the flux, and a spline per slot would need a second smoothing weight to choose.
- LightGBM (EXP-008): one more numeric column, configuration unchanged.

## Choice made on 2023 only (D13)
One encoding choice, between two candidates:
- `slot`: the forecast flux at the slot itself;
- `daily`: the mean forecast flux of the delivery day, the same value for its 48 slots (a sunny or cloudy day signal).

Rule: same inner walk-forward on 2023 as for part (b), EXP-005 GAM without the input (must reproduce 1057.0 MW) and with each candidate. Choose the lowest 2023 MAE; within 1.0 MW of each other, take `daily` (the simpler one). The choice is used for both experts and for (a)+(b). The 2024 runs are made whatever the 2023 result. For (a)+(b) the school share keeps its frozen encoding `working_days`; no new choice is made.

## What I will report for 2024 (nothing from 2025)
- (a): each expert plus `solar_wm2` against its reference (EXP-005 recomputed in the run, saved EXP-008 forecast), with the same tables as part (b).
- (a)+(b): each expert plus both inputs. Its reference is the (a) run of the same expert, so the single change is the school share, added on top of the solar input. I also report it against EXP-005 or EXP-008, to give the total gain of both inputs.

- ML-Poly aggregation (EXP-009): rerun only if an expert gains significantly against its reference on the 7-day block.
- No 2025 read: the script asserts that consumption, temperature, radiation and every forecast end before 2025-01-01 Paris.
